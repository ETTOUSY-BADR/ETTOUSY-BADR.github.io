02

Bridging Non-Stationarity & Deep Learning

An ENSEA / ETIS research framework combining econometric tests for changing temporal distributions with a PyTorch implementation of TimeBridge for long horizon forecasting.

01 · Context and question

How can classical diagnostics help interpret deep forecasting models when short term and long term temporal structure both change?

Connecting classical statistical diagnostics with TimeBridge representations.

02 · Approach

  1. 01

    Applied ADF, KPSS, Priestley Subba Rao, spectral invariance and Engle Granger diagnostics to temporal datasets.

  2. 02

    Implemented TimeBridge components including integrated attention, cointegrated attention, patch embedding and downsampling.

  3. 03

    Built dataset, forecasting and evaluation pipelines for ETT, Weather, Solar, Electricity and Traffic series.

03 · Structure and workflow

01

Diagnose

Characterise stationarity, changing spectra and cointegration before modelling.

02

Represent

Separate short term variation from long term dependencies in learned attention.

03

Forecast

Train controlled long horizon configurations across public temporal datasets.

04

Compare

Record error metrics and relate behaviour back to statistical diagnostics.

04 · Evidence and material

The public result log records forecasting runs on ETTm1, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm2, Weather, Solar, Electricity and Traffic configurations, together with reported MSE, RMSE, MAPE and MSPE fields.

05 · Outcome and learning

The repository turns the study into a reusable Python framework. Statistical test notebooks, synthetic data support, a clean TimeBridge implementation and dataset specific experiment scripts live in one structure.

06 · Scope and limitations

The public result file records individual runs but does not provide a complete controlled baseline table or statistical comparison. This case study therefore makes no claim of leading performance. Conclusions remain sensitive to splits, horizons and metric conventions.

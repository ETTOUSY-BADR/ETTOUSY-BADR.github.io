01 · Context and question

How can heterogeneous public data improve apartment valuation without leaking future information into historical predictions?

Leakage-aware apartment valuation under a frozen chronological protocol.

02 · Approach

  1. 01

    Resolved DVF transactions against BAN addresses and BDNB buildings, then joined dated DPE records and neighbourhood context.

  2. 02

    Used 2021 to 2023 for development, 2024 for model selection and 2025 as a frozen final evaluation cohort.

  3. 03

    Compared enrichment ablations and model families while auditing row identity, DPE timing and reproducible artifacts.

03 · Structure and workflow

01

Resolve

Link transactions to canonical addresses and buildings with explicit match audits.

02

Enrich

Add point-in-time energy data and carefully labelled retrospective context.

03

Validate

Select features and models on 2024 without touching the final 2025 cohort.

04

Audit

Verify leakage, artifacts, uncertainty and segment-level behaviour.

04 · Evidence and material

The selected Phase 3 CatBoost model was refitted on data from 2021 to 2024 and evaluated on 28,330 sales from 2025. The public report compares full enrichment with building, dated DPE, neighbourhood and frozen Phase 2 references.

05 · Outcome and learning

The public Phase 3 report records EUR 93,136 MAE, 11.70% median absolute percentage error and R² of 0.8679. MAE improves by EUR 3,293, or 3.41%, over the frozen Phase 2 reference. The paired 95% bootstrap interval is between EUR 2,735 and EUR 3,846.

06 · Scope and limitations

Dated DPE and comparable sale features use information available at each historical date. Several building and neighbourhood fields are retrospective static reconstructions. Floor and elevator data remain unavailable rather than being imputed, and prospective deployment requires a newly validated cohort.
